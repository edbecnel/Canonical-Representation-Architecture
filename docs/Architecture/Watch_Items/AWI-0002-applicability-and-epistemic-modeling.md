# AWI-0002 — Applicability and Epistemic Modeling

> **Status:** Open
> **Date:** 2026-08-12
> **Owner:** Architecture Team

## Summary

Open architectural questions concerning whether canonicality, context, and applicability are distinct properties; how subjective, conditional, and contested knowledge is represented; and what epistemic status modeling CRA requires.

## Scope

This Watch Item consolidates the following related concerns from [AI Canonical Knowledge Interaction and Evolution](../../Discovery_Records/AI_Canonical_Knowledge_Interaction_and_Evolution.md):

- **Canonicality vs applicability** — canonical knowledge may be valid yet inappropriate for a situation
- **Context vs applicability** — meaning frame vs conditions for use
- **Relationship to scope, relationships, qualification, and epistemic status**
- **Subjective, conditional, contested, and probabilistic knowledge**
- **Disagreement as canonical knowledge** — preserving alternatives without artificial resolution
- **Applicability encoding limits** — when applicability cannot be deterministically encoded

## Why Watch, Not Specification Now

- Applicability is an architectural question, not a wording patch to the Canonical Knowledge definition
- Context/applicability distinction requires structural analysis before CRA-0001 evaluation
- CRA-0001 obligation #4 permits governed uncertainty but does not define representation mechanics
- May depend on canonical relationship model expressiveness

## Foundational Questions for CRA-0001 Evaluation

Recorded in [Knowledge Interaction/Evolution Workstream](../../../Development/Knowledge_Interaction_Evolution_Workstream.md):

- Is "does not imply universal applicability" sufficient, or is a distinct applicability architecture required?
- Are context and applicability distinct CRA concepts?

**CRA-0001 must not be modified normatively until these questions are evaluated.**

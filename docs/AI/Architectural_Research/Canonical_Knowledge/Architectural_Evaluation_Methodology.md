# Architectural Evaluation Methodology

## Purpose

This document records the methodology adopted for evaluating candidate definitions of **Canonical Knowledge** during the architectural development of the Canonical Representation Architecture (CRA).

It was created following a structured architectural inquiry involving multiple independent AI systems and subsequent engineering analysis.

The purpose of this document is to establish how candidate definitions shall be evaluated, rather than to define Canonical Knowledge itself.

---

# Fundamental Observation

During the architectural inquiry it became apparent that many plausible definitions of *Canonical Knowledge* exist.

Some definitions emphasize:

- meaning,
- semantics,
- assertions,
- relationships,
- records,
- facts,
- identity,
- equivalence,
- preservation,
- governance,
- or other architectural viewpoints.

Many of these definitions are internally consistent.

However, the existence of multiple reasonable definitions demonstrated that the question:

> **"What is Canonical Knowledge?"**

cannot be answered in isolation.

The answer depends upon the architectural problem being solved.

---

# Architectural Principle

The Canonical Representation Architecture does not seek to define Canonical Knowledge as a philosophical, linguistic, or epistemological concept.

Instead, CRA seeks to define Canonical Knowledge as an architectural concept whose purpose is to enable the goals of the Canonical Representation Architecture.

Accordingly, the architectural definition shall be derived from the requirements of the architecture itself rather than from existing terminology or external consensus.

---

# Engineering Methodology

Candidate definitions shall be evaluated according to the following process.

## Step 1 — Identify the Architectural Problem

Before defining Canonical Knowledge, identify the architectural problem that CRA is attempting to solve.

The definition must serve the architecture rather than exist independently of it.

---

## Step 2 — Identify Architectural Requirements

Determine the capabilities that Canonical Knowledge must provide in order for CRA to achieve its objectives.

Examples may include:

- preservation across evolving representations,
- representation independence,
- stable identity,
- semantic fidelity,
- derivation of multiple representations,
- provenance,
- versioning,
- traceability,
- contextual preservation,
- support for AI reasoning,
- support for human understanding.

These requirements are expected to evolve during architectural development.

---

## Step 3 — Evaluate Candidate Concepts

Each candidate concept discovered through research shall be evaluated according to one primary question:

> **Does this concept help CRA achieve its architectural objectives?**

Concepts that do not materially contribute to the architecture should not become part of the canonical definition, regardless of how widely accepted they may be elsewhere.

---

## Step 4 — Evaluate Against Architectural Requirements

Every candidate definition should be evaluated against the complete set of architectural requirements.

Questions include:

- Does the definition preserve representation independence?
- Does it support derivation?
- Does it support identity?
- Does it support long-term preservation?
- Does it avoid unnecessary implementation assumptions?
- Does it remain technology independent?
- Does it support future architectural evolution?

A definition that fails these tests should be rejected or refined.

---

## Step 5 — Engineer the Definition

The final architectural definition should be engineered from the requirements of CRA.

It should not be selected by:

- popularity,
- majority opinion,
- existing industry terminology,
- AI consensus,
- or philosophical preference.

The resulting definition should represent the smallest architectural concept capable of supporting the complete Canonical Representation Architecture.

---

# Role of Independent AI Reviews

The independent AI responses collected during this inquiry serve as architectural research inputs.

They contribute:

- alternative viewpoints,
- hidden assumptions,
- terminology,
- examples,
- counterexamples,
- architectural critiques,
- and potential design directions.

They do **not** determine the final architecture.

Architectural decisions remain the responsibility of the CRA engineering process.

---

# Guiding Principle

The central question of this inquiry is **not**:

> *What does the world believe Canonical Knowledge is?*

Nor is it:

> *Which AI produced the best definition?*

Instead, the governing question is:

> **What must Canonical Knowledge be in order for the Canonical Representation Architecture to achieve its intended purpose?**

The answer to that question shall determine the architectural definition adopted by CRA.

---

# Relationship to CRA

This document is informative rather than normative.

It records the methodology used to evaluate architectural concepts during the discovery of the Canonical Representation Architecture.

Normative definitions shall appear only within the official CRA architectural specifications.

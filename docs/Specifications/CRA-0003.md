## Purpose

This specification establishes **representation fidelity** requirements for the Canonical Representation Architecture (CRA).

CRA-0003 operationalizes [CRA-0001](CRA-0001.md) FP-1 (Separation of Canonical Knowledge and Derived Representations) and the fidelity obligation in the Canonical Knowledge definition by defining normative requirements for derivation, distinction preservation, and regeneration of derived representations.

This document fulfills the fidelity mechanics deferred from CRA-0001 and addresses open question **#3** recorded in [Canonical Knowledge Conclusions](../../AI/Architectural_Research/Canonical_Knowledge/Conclusions.md): what constitutes an acceptable derived representation of canonical knowledge.

## Scope

### In scope

- Normative definition of representation fidelity and derivation
- Requirements for fidelity-preserving and lossy derived representations
- Operational boundary between representational change and semantic change (building on [CRA-0002](CRA-0002.md))
- Regeneration obligations when derived representations are replaced
- Navigation as a derived representation subject to fidelity rules
- Fidelity-level conformance expectations

### Out of scope

The following topics are intentionally deferred to later CRA specifications or program work:

- Validation tooling, scoring frameworks, and certification procedures
- Publication pipeline and repository layout implementation
- Full canonical relationship model beyond navigation fidelity
- Domain-specific evidence promotion rules
- Full controlled vocabulary and glossary

## Normative Definitions

The following terms build on [CRA-0001](CRA-0001.md) and [CRA-0002](CRA-0002.md) vocabulary. Terms defined in prior specifications are used here without redefinition.

### Committed Distinction

A semantic, epistemic, relational, or structural commitment that scope governance treats as irreducible for a given canonical artifact and version. Committed distinctions MAY include exact wording, dispute structure, supersession markers, epistemic status, provenance bindings, and canonical relationships as defined by scope policy.

### Derivation

The production of a derived representation from one or more canonical artifacts, versions, or other derived representations governed within a scope.

### Representation Fidelity

The degree to which a derived representation preserves all committed distinctions required by scope governance for the canonical artifact and version it expresses.

A derived representation is **fidelity-preserving** when it preserves all such distinctions. It is **lossy** when it omits or alters one or more committed distinctions.

### Fidelity Reference

The canonical artifact and version against which a derived representation's fidelity is evaluated. The fidelity reference MUST be the designated canonical source, not another derived representation.

### Regeneration

The replacement of a derived representation by a newly produced derived representation intended to express the same canonical artifact and version.

## Fidelity Requirements

The following requirements are normative for any architecture, repository, or system that claims conformance with CRA at the representation fidelity level. They operationalize CRA-0001 FP-1, FP-3, and FP-5 in conjunction with [CRA-0002](CRA-0002.md) identity requirements.

### RF-1 — Canonical Fidelity Reference

An adopter MUST evaluate the fidelity of a derived representation against its designated canonical artifact and version.

An adopter MUST NOT treat a derived representation as the authoritative fidelity reference for canonical knowledge.

**Rationale:** Operationalizes FP-1. Prevents derivation chains that elevate derived artifacts to canonical standing.

### RF-2 — Distinction Preservation

A fidelity-preserving derived representation MUST preserve all committed distinctions that scope governance requires for the referenced canonical artifact and version.

An adopter MUST be able to identify which distinctions are committed for a given canonical artifact and version within scope.

**Rationale:** Core of semantic integrity; fidelity is evaluated against scope-committed distinctions, not against informal notions of "sameness."

### RF-3 — Lossy Derivation Boundaries

A lossy derived representation MUST be identifiable as lossy relative to its fidelity reference.

An adopter MUST NOT treat a lossy derived representation as a substitute for its fidelity reference canonical artifact and version.

Lossy derived representations include, but are not limited to: summaries, embeddings, ranked search indexes, inference products, and aggregated projections — unless scope governance explicitly designates a specific lossy form as fidelity-preserving for a defined purpose.

**Rationale:** Prevents silent canonical substitution by convenient or lossy views.

### RF-4 — Representational Change Fidelity

When a change affects only the encoding, format, publication channel, or organizational locator of a canonical artifact without governed semantic change (representational change per [CRA-0002](CRA-0002.md)), any new derived representation of that artifact and version MUST be fidelity-preserving.

Representational change MUST NOT alter the canonical identifier or version per CRA-0002 IM-4 and IM-5.

**Rationale:** Operationalizes the CRA-0002 representational/semantic change boundary at the fidelity layer.

### RF-5 — Semantic Change and Versioning

When governed semantic change occurs, the adopter MUST create a new version or lineage event per CRA-0002 IM-5 before treating updated content as the current fidelity reference.

A derived representation of a prior version MUST continue to reference that prior version as its fidelity reference unless explicitly regenerated against a different version through governed process.

**Rationale:** Links fidelity obligations to CRA-0002 versioning; prevents silent semantic overwrite via derived representation replacement.

### RF-6 — Regeneration Integrity

When a derived representation is discarded and regenerated, the regenerated representation MUST be fidelity-preserving for the same fidelity reference, or MUST be explicitly governed and identified as a new lossy derivation.

An adopter MUST NOT use regeneration as a mechanism to silently alter committed distinctions.

**Rationale:** Derived representations are replaceable carriers (FP-1); regeneration must not become ungoverned semantic mutation.

### RF-7 — Navigation as Derived Representation

Navigation structures — including tables of contents, browse hierarchies, hyperlink graphs, and presentation order — MUST be treated as derived representations of canonical relationships.

An adopter MUST NOT treat navigation structures as the authoritative definition of relationships among canonical artifacts.

Navigation structures SHOULD be regenerable from canonical relationship commitments without collapsing committed distinctions.

**Rationale:** Operationalizes FP-3 at the fidelity layer. Relationship identity beyond navigation fidelity may be addressed in future specifications.

## Derivation and Change Classification

An adopter MUST classify changes affecting canonical artifacts according to the following model, consistent with [CRA-0002](CRA-0002.md):

| Change type | Identity | Version | Fidelity obligation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Representational change** | Unchanged | Unchanged | New or updated derived representation MUST be fidelity-preserving for the same version |
| **Semantic change** | Unchanged | New version or lineage event | New version becomes fidelity reference for forward-looking derivations; prior version fidelity obligations per IM-6 |
| **Derived representation replacement** | Unchanged | Unchanged | Regeneration MUST satisfy RF-6 |

## Architectural Obligations

In addition to RF-1 through RF-7, adopters MUST satisfy the following obligations at the representation fidelity level:

1. An adopter MUST maintain a distinguishable canonical layer separate from derived representations (FP-1).
2. An adopter MUST document or otherwise make recoverable which committed distinctions apply within each scope.
3. An adopter MUST be able to determine whether a given artifact is canonical, a fidelity-preserving derived representation, or a lossy derived representation (extending CRA-0002 IM-3).
4. An adopter MAY maintain multiple fidelity-preserving derived representations of the same canonical artifact and version for different consumption purposes.

## Conformance

Conformance with CRA-0003 at the representation fidelity level means an adopter can demonstrate the following in addition to [CRA-0001](CRA-0001.md) principles-level and [CRA-0002](CRA-0002.md) identity-level conformance:

| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| Fidelity reference | Are derived representations evaluated against canonical artifacts and versions, not against other derived representations? |
| Distinction preservation | Can the adopter identify committed distinctions and evaluate whether a derived representation preserves them? |
| Lossy identification | Are lossy derived representations identifiable and prevented from substituting for canonical sources? |
| Representational change | Are encoding or locator changes handled as fidelity-preserving re-derivation without semantic version change? |
| Regeneration integrity | Does regeneration preserve committed distinctions or explicitly record lossy re-derivation? |
| Navigation separation | Are navigation structures treated as derived views rather than authoritative relationship definitions? |

This specification does not define scoring frameworks, validation tooling, or certification procedures.

## Rationale

CRA-0001 established that canonical knowledge and derived representations are architecturally distinct and that canonical status establishes a preservation and fidelity obligation within scope. CRA-0002 defined identity, versioning, and the boundary between representational and semantic change but deferred fidelity rules.

Engineering research converges on a single architectural requirement: **fidelity means preserving scope-committed distinctions**, with the canonical artifact as the evaluation reference — not convenience, popularity, or the most accessible encoding.

CRA-0003 normativizes that requirement in technology-independent terms so adopters can create, replace, and regenerate derived representations without silent loss of semantic integrity.

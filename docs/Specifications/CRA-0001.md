## Purpose

This specification establishes the **foundational architectural principles** of the Canonical Representation Architecture (CRA).

CRA-0001 is the constitutional layer from which all subsequent CRA specifications derive. It normativizes the invariant principles governing how canonical knowledge is created, identified, represented, transformed, published, consumed, and evolved while preserving semantic integrity.

This document fulfills the first architectural initiative identified in [CRA-0000](../../CRA-0000.md) §9: the definition of fundamental principles governing canonical representations.

## Scope

### In scope

- Normative definition of Canonical Knowledge
- Five foundational principles (FP-1 through FP-5) with RFC 2119 requirements
- Minimal architectural vocabulary required to state those principles
- High-level architectural obligations for adopters
- Lightweight conformance expectations at the principles level
- Explicit deferrals to future CRA specifications

### Out of scope

The following topics are intentionally deferred to later CRA specifications:

- Identifier syntax, equivalence rules, and versioning mechanics (identity model)
- Detailed representation fidelity rules and validation procedures
- Publication pipeline, repository intelligence, and interaction layer responsibilities
- Reference implementation requirements
- Full controlled vocabulary and glossary
- Domain-specific evidence promotion rules (for example, electronics or AI-assisted analysis workflows)

## Normative Definitions

The following terms are normative within CRA. Additional vocabulary MAY emerge in subsequent specifications.

### Canonical Knowledge

**Concise definition:**

> Canonical Knowledge is governed, scope-bounded semantic content whose identity and preservation an architecture commits to across representational change — not any particular file, format, index, or derived artifact.

**Expanded definition:**

> Within a declared scope, Canonical Knowledge is the identity-bearing semantic content that a designated authority has admitted for preservation and governance, together with the provenance, epistemic or normative status, and relationships necessary to preserve its meaning when documents, storage systems, publications, interfaces, indexes, and other representations are created, replaced, or discarded. Canonical status establishes a preservation and fidelity obligation within that scope. It does not imply truth, completeness, immutability, or universal authority.

### Canonical Artifact

The smallest governed unit of Canonical Knowledge within a scope. A canonical artifact is the unit to which identity, governance designation, preservation obligations, and relationships apply.

This specification does not prescribe how canonical artifacts are stored, encoded, or named.

### Derived Representation

Any encoding, publication, index, navigation view, inference product, or other artifact produced from Canonical Knowledge for storage, presentation, search, consumption, or reasoning. Derived representations are architecturally distinct from Canonical Knowledge even when they are the only visible form of that knowledge in a given system.

### Scope

A declared boundary within which canonical designation applies and within which preservation and governance obligations are meaningful. Scope MAY be defined by repository, program, organization, standard, domain, version, time interval, or other explicit architectural boundary.

### Governance Designation

An explicit act by a defined authority or governance process that confers canonical status on content within a scope. Canonicality is not an intrinsic property of content, format popularity, storage location, or reference frequency.

## Foundational Principles

The following principles are normative for any architecture, repository, or system that claims conformance with CRA at the principles level.

### FP-1 — Separation of Canonical Knowledge and Derived Representations

Canonical Knowledge and Derived Representations are architecturally distinct.

An adopter MUST treat Canonical Knowledge as separate from the representations through which it is expressed, published, indexed, navigated, or inferred.

Derived Representations MAY be created, replaced, or discarded without changing the semantic identity of designated Canonical Knowledge, subject to fidelity obligations defined in future CRA specifications.

**Rationale:** Recurring engineering failures treat a particular encoding as the thing worth preserving, then lose meaning, identity, or history when representations change. CRA-0000 §6.1 records this discovery descriptively; this principle normativizes it.

### FP-2 — Identity Independent of Organization

Each canonical artifact MUST retain stable semantic identity across physical location, repository structure, filename, publication format, storage technology, and future reorganization.

Organizational change MUST NOT be treated as semantic change unless an explicit governance act declares otherwise within scope.

**Rationale:** CRA-0000 §6.2 establishes that identity must remain independent of organization. Without stable identity, preservation across representational evolution is impossible.

### FP-3 — Relationships Independent of Navigation

Relationships among canonical artifacts exist independently of navigation mechanisms, link syntax, presentation order, or user-interface affordances.

Navigation MUST be understood as a derived view of relationships, not as part of Canonical Knowledge itself.

An adopter MUST NOT treat navigation paths, hyperlinks, or browse hierarchies as the authoritative definition of relationships among canonical artifacts.

**Rationale:** CRA-0000 §6.3 records that engineering knowledge contains relationships that exist independent of how they are navigated. Conflating navigation with relationship structure binds canonical meaning to presentation technology.

### FP-4 — Scope-Bounded Governed Designation

Canonical status MUST be conferred within an explicit scope by a defined governance process or authority.

An adopter MUST NOT treat content as canonical solely because it is widely copied, stored in a durable location, referenced frequently, or expressed in a preferred format.

Canonical designation MUST be recoverable: adopters MUST be able to identify what authority conferred canonical status, within what scope, and under what governance rules.

**Rationale:** Canonicality is a preservation and authority commitment within a boundary, not global truth. Architectures that treat popularity or format longevity as canonicality collapse governance into accident.

### FP-5 — Preservation Across Representational Evolution

An adopter MUST commit to preserving designated Canonical Knowledge across representational change within scope.

Silent loss, ungoverned corruption, or unrecoverable ambiguity of designated canonical content within scope constitutes architectural failure.

Preservation MUST include sufficient provenance and epistemic status to interpret canonical content across time and across changes in representation.

**Rationale:** CRA's central objective is to preserve canonical knowledge as a stable engineering asset while representations and technologies evolve. CRA-0000 closing reflection states this objective explicitly.

## Architectural Obligations

In addition to FP-1 through FP-5, adopters MUST satisfy the following obligations at the principles level. Mechanisms are deferred to future specifications.

1. An adopter MUST maintain a distinguishable canonical layer separate from Derived Representations.
2. An adopter MUST define scope and governance authority for canonical designation.
3. An adopter MUST preserve provenance and epistemic status sufficient to interpret canonical content across representational change.
4. An adopter MAY designate uncertainty, disagreement, competing interpretations, and superseded claims as Canonical Knowledge when explicitly governed within scope.

### Evidence artifacts

Primary evidence records (observations, measurements, analysis outputs, hypotheses, and similar artifacts) MAY become Canonical Knowledge when explicitly governed and promoted through a defined process.

Raw or derived evidence does not become canonical by storage, production, or repetition alone. Detailed promotion rules are deferred to future CRA specifications and domain-specific governance.

## Conformance

Conformance with CRA-0001 at the principles level means an adopter can demonstrate the following:

| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| Canonical layer | Can the adopter identify its canonical layer separately from derived representations? |
| Governed designation | Is canonical status conferred through an explicit governance process within a declared scope? |
| Identity | Is semantic identity preserved across organizational and representational change? |
| Preservation commitment | Is there an explicit commitment to preserve designated canonical knowledge within scope? |
| Relationship separation | Are relationships among canonical artifacts defined independently of navigation mechanisms? |

This specification does not define scoring frameworks, validation tooling, or certification procedures. Those belong in future CRA validation methodology specifications.

## Rationale

CRA emerged from repeated independent engineering efforts that converged on the same architectural problems: preserving knowledge across changing representations, maintaining stable identity, and separating authoritative content from presentation and tooling.

CRA-0000 §6 records these observations descriptively. Canonical Knowledge research tested definitions against CRA's architectural purpose rather than external terminology or AI consensus.

CRA-0001 normativizes the smallest set of principles that enable that purpose across domains and technologies. Identity mechanics, fidelity rules, and domain-specific governance are necessary but belong in subsequent specifications so that this constitutional layer remains stable, technology-independent, and adoptable.

## Purpose

This specification establishes the **identity model** of the Canonical Representation Architecture (CRA).

CRA-0002 operationalizes [CRA-0001](CRA-0001.md) FP-2 (Identity Independent of Organization) by defining normative requirements for canonical identifiers, identity equivalence, and versioning semantics for canonical artifacts within a scope.

This document fulfills the identity mechanics deferred from CRA-0001 and addresses the open question recorded in [Canonical Knowledge Conclusions](../../AI/Architectural_Research/Canonical_Knowledge/Conclusions.md): what identifiers, equivalence rules, and versioning semantics bind canonical artifacts across time.

## Scope

### In scope

- Normative identity requirements for canonical artifacts within a scope
- Identifier requirements and syntax profile (technology-independent)
- Equivalence and non-equivalence rules
- Versioning, lineage, and supersession semantics
- Distinction between identity, organizational locators, and derived representation references
- Identity-level conformance expectations

### Out of scope

The following topics are intentionally deferred to later CRA specifications:

- Representation fidelity rules and validation procedures (CRA-0003)
- Publication pipeline, navigation, or repository layout
- Identifier registry implementation technology
- Global cross-scope identity federation
- Full controlled vocabulary and glossary

## Normative Definitions

The following terms build on [CRA-0001](CRA-0001.md) vocabulary. Terms defined in CRA-0001 are used here without redefinition.

### Canonical Identifier

A stable, scope-qualified name assigned to a canonical artifact at governance designation time. A canonical identifier is independent of physical location, repository structure, filename, publication format, and storage technology.

### Organizational Locator

Any address, path, URL, filename, commit reference, database key, index position, or other mechanism that locates a canonical artifact or its derived representation within a storage or publication system. An organizational locator is architecturally distinct from a canonical identifier.

### Identity Binding

The governed association between a canonical identifier and a canonical artifact within a scope, established at designation time and maintained across representational change.

### Identity Equivalence

The determination that two manifestations refer to the same canonical artifact under the equivalence rules defined in this specification.

### Version

A governed state of a canonical artifact at a point in time. The canonical identifier persists across versions; each version records committed semantic content as designated within scope.

### Lineage

The ordered history of versions and governance events (amendment, supersession, split, merge) associated with a canonical identifier within a scope.

### Supersession

A governed relationship in which a later version or canonical artifact replaces the authoritative standing of an earlier version or artifact without erasing the identity or referential integrity of the superseded state.

## Identity Requirements

The following requirements are normative for any architecture, repository, or system that claims conformance with CRA at the identity model level. They operationalize CRA-0001 FP-2, FP-4, and FP-5.

### IM-1 — Identifier Assignment

Every canonical artifact within a scope MUST have exactly one canonical identifier assigned at governance designation time.

An adopter MUST NOT designate a canonical artifact without assigning a canonical identifier.

**Rationale:** Without an identity anchor at designation, preservation across representational change cannot be guaranteed.

### IM-2 — Independence from Organizational Locators

Canonical identifiers MUST NOT be defined solely by organizational locators.

An adopter MUST maintain canonical identifiers separately from paths, URLs, filenames, storage keys, and other locators that MAY change when content is relocated or reformatted.

**Rationale:** CRA-0000 §6.2; operationalizes FP-2. Organizational locators MAY be used as derived references but MUST NOT serve as the sole identity mechanism.

### IM-3 — Equivalence Determination

An adopter MUST be able to determine, for any pair of manifestations within scope, whether they refer to:

1. the same canonical artifact (same identity, same version),
2. a different version of the same canonical artifact,
3. a different canonical artifact, or
4. a derived representation of canonical knowledge.

**Rationale:** Without equivalence determination, preservation is indistinguishable from copying text.

### IM-4 — Relocation Invariance

Organizational relocation, reformatting, or republication without a governed semantic change MUST NOT alter the canonical identifier or identity equivalence of the affected canonical artifact.

An adopter MUST NOT treat organizational change as semantic change unless an explicit governance act declares otherwise within scope.

**Rationale:** Direct operationalization of FP-2.

### IM-5 — Governed Semantic Evolution

Semantic change to a designated canonical artifact within scope MUST be expressed as a new version or governed lineage event.

An adopter MUST NOT silently overwrite the semantic content of a designated canonical artifact in place.

**Rationale:** FP-5 preservation obligation; silent semantic overwrite constitutes ungoverned corruption.

### IM-6 — Historical Reference Integrity

Superseded versions MUST remain resolvable by canonical identifier for references that depend on historical standing.

An adopter MUST NOT destroy the ability to resolve a superseded version when later versions or superseding artifacts exist.

**Rationale:** Contracts, standards, and audit trails depend on identity persistence across supersession.

### IM-7 — Governed Split, Merge, and Cross-Artifact Equivalence

Split, merge, or declaration that two canonical artifacts are equivalent MUST require an explicit governance act within scope.

An adopter MUST NOT infer split, merge, or cross-artifact equivalence from representation similarity, storage co-location, or automated deduplication alone.

**Rationale:** FP-4 governed designation; prevents accidental collapse of distinct canonical commitments.

## Identifier Syntax Profile

CRA-0002 specifies a requirement profile for canonical identifiers. It does not mandate a single global string format.

An adopter's canonical identifiers MUST satisfy the following:

| Property | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Opacity** | Identifiers MUST be opaque with respect to storage layout; uniqueness MUST NOT depend on path-embedded semantics |
| **Scope qualification** | Identifiers MUST be meaningful within a declared scope; the same opaque string in different scopes is not assumed equivalent |
| **Immutability** | Identifiers SHOULD be immutable once assigned to a canonical artifact |
| **Format flexibility** | Adopters MAY encode identifiers as URIs, UUIDs, namespaced codes, or other formats provided IM-1 through IM-7 are satisfied |

If URI syntax is used for a canonical identifier, the URI MUST be treated as an identity name, not as proof of network location or retrievability.

CRA does not mandate SIP `@path` or `sipi:` schemes. The Semantic Identity Protocol remains an informative reference implementation for internet semantic addressing per [CRA-0000](../../CRA-0000.md) §7.2.

## Versioning and Lineage

### Persistent identity

The canonical identifier of a canonical artifact persists across all versions of that artifact within scope.

### Version states

Each version records the committed semantic content of a canonical artifact at a point in time as designated within scope. Version identifiers MAY be explicit (for example, version numbers, timestamps, or revision labels) or implicit in lineage records, provided an adopter can satisfy IM-3.

### Representational change versus semantic change

| Change type | Identity impact | Version impact |
|---|---|---|
| **Representational change** | Unchanged | Unchanged (same version, new derived representation) |
| **Semantic change** | Unchanged | New version or lineage event required |

Detailed fidelity rules for derived representations are defined in [CRA-0003](CRA-0003.md).

### Supersession

Supersession changes which version or artifact holds authoritative standing within scope. A superseded version or artifact retains its canonical identifier and MUST remain resolvable per IM-6.

### Lineage events

The following governance events MUST be recorded in lineage when they occur within scope:

- **Amendment** — creation of a new version with governed semantic change
- **Supersession** — replacement of authoritative standing by a later version or artifact
- **Split** — one canonical artifact becomes two or more distinct canonical artifacts
- **Merge** — two or more canonical artifacts are governed as a single canonical artifact or equivalence class

## Architectural Obligations

In addition to IM-1 through IM-7, adopters MUST satisfy the following obligations at the identity model level:

1. An adopter MUST maintain a registry or equivalent mechanism that binds canonical identifiers to canonical artifacts within each scope.
2. An adopter MUST record lineage for governed semantic changes, supersession, split, and merge events.
3. An adopter MUST distinguish organizational locators from canonical identifiers in all identity-aware operations.
4. An adopter MAY maintain multiple organizational locators for the same canonical artifact without affecting identity equivalence.

## Conformance

Conformance with CRA-0002 at the identity model level means an adopter can demonstrate the following in addition to [CRA-0001](CRA-0001.md) principles-level conformance:

| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| Identifier assignment | Can the adopter assign stable identifiers at designation independent of locators? |
| Equivalence determination | Can the adopter distinguish same artifact, different version, different artifact, and derived representation? |
| Relocation invariance | Does relocation without a governance act preserve identity? |
| Governed evolution | Are semantic changes versioned or lineage-recorded rather than silently overwritten? |
| Historical reference | Can superseded versions still be resolved by identity? |
| Locator separation | Are organizational locators maintained separately from canonical identifiers? |

This specification does not define scoring frameworks, validation tooling, or certification procedures.

## Rationale

CRA-0001 established that canonical artifacts MUST retain stable semantic identity independent of organization (FP-2) but deferred the mechanics of identifiers, equivalence, and versioning.

Engineering research, SIP's separation of semantic identity from transport location, and recurring failure modes (path-as-identity, content-hash-as-identity, silent overwrite) converge on a single architectural requirement: identity must be an explicit, governed, scope-qualified commitment — not an accident of storage or encoding.

CRA-0002 normativizes that requirement in technology-independent terms so adopters can implement identity-aware preservation without binding to a particular repository layout, URI scheme, or identifier format.
